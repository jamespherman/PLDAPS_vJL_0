# gSac_4factors: Complete Task Description

This document describes the behavioral design of the `tasks/gSac_4factors/` task:
what the subject sees, what they must do, how they are rewarded, how trial
conditions are scheduled, and what counts as a correct or an error trial.
Field-level definitions of the saved data are in
`data_dictionaries/4factors_data_dictionary.md` and are not repeated here.

All parameter values below are the **defaults in `gSac_4factors_settings.m`**.
Most can be changed from the GUI during a session, and the values actually
used on each trial are saved in `p.trVars`.

---

## 1. Quick answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is this a visually or memory-guided saccade task? | **Memory-guided on every trial.** The target flashes for 400 ms, and the go signal comes 100–600 ms after it turns off (§5.2). |
| How many target locations are there? | **4.** Location 1 is set by the experimenter, and locations 2–4 are 90° rotations of it at the same eccentricity (§3). Only one target is shown per trial. There is no choice. |
| Are reward and probability manipulated on one side or both? | **Reward: both sides at once, in opposite directions.** At any moment one left/right hemifield (both of its locations) gives high reward and the other gives low reward. The assignment swaps every half-block. **Probability: a single location, not a side.** One location is high-probability (location 1 in block 1, location 3 in block 2), and the other three, including its same-hemifield neighbour, are low-probability. |
| Is the subject cued about reward size or probability? | **No.** Neither is signalled on the subject's screen. The subject can only learn them from experience within a block or half-block. The green "high-reward" frame is drawn only on the experimenter display. |
| Is the subject cued about salience or stimulus category? | **Indirectly, yes.** The background colour is set per trial by stimulus type and is visible from the first frame of the trial, before the target appears. Grey means an image trial, and each of the four coloured backgrounds maps to exactly one bullseye condition (§4.3). |
| What is the response? | One saccade to the remembered target location after the fixation point turns off, followed by holding gaze there for 200–300 ms. The joystick is not used (`passJoy = 1`, §6.1). |
| Is a choice ever made? | No. Every trial has exactly one target, and the only outcomes are correct or one of several error types. |
| What happens after an error? | A low tone plays and there is **no timeout**. The same condition stays in the pool and is redrawn later in the same half-block (not immediately) (§7.4). |
| What is the success feedback? | Juice reward (no tone), plus the target reappearing at its location during the post-saccade hold period. |

## 2. Overview and the four factors

On each trial the subject fixates a central point. A peripheral target flashes
once at one of four locations, and the subject keeps fixating through a short
delay. When the fixation point disappears, the subject saccades to the
remembered target location, holds gaze there, and receives a juice reward.

Four factors are manipulated:

| Factor | Levels | Timescale of manipulation | Trial-array column |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Stimulus identity / salience** | Face image, non-face image, bullseye high-salience, bullseye low-salience (each bullseye in two hue variants) | Trial-by-trial, randomly interleaved | `stimType` (1–6), `salience`, `targetColor` |
| **2. Reward magnitude** | High (350 ms solenoid) / Low (160 ms) | Changes every **half-block**, by left/right hemifield | `reward` (1 = high, 2 = low) |
| **3. Target location probability** | High-probability location (5× base rate) / low-probability locations (1×) | Changes every **block** (2 half-blocks) | implicit in counts; see §7 |
| **4. Target location** | Locations 1–4 | Trial-by-trial, with the probabilities from factor 3 | `targetLocIdx` |

Factor 1 is **nested**, not fully crossed. Salience (high/low) exists only for
bullseye targets (`salience = 0` for images), and face vs. non-face exists
only for image targets.

## 3. Spatial layout

### 3.1 Fixation
- Fixation point at `(fixDegX, fixDegY) = (0, 0)`, the screen centre.
- Shape: a **black square outline**, 32 px across (`fixPointRadius = 16` px) with a 6 px line. Its size in degrees depends on the rig's pixels per degree.

### 3.2 The four target locations (`nextParams.m → setLocations`)
The experimenter sets a **base position** `(targDegX_base, targDegY_base)`,
default `(17, 3)` deg, from the GUI. The four locations all have the base
eccentricity and are generated by rotating the base in 90° steps:

- If the base lies in quadrant 1 or 3 (x and y have the same sign), the rotation step is **−90°** (clockwise).
- If the base lies in quadrant 2 or 4, the rotation step is **+90°** (counter-clockwise).

| Location | Rule | Default position (deg) | Polar angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | base | (17.0, 3.0) | 10° |
| 2 | base rotated 1 step | (3.0, −17.0) | −80° (280°) |
| 3 | base rotated 2 steps = **diametrically opposite loc 1** | (−17.0, −3.0) | 190° |
| 4 | base rotated 3 steps = **diametrically opposite loc 2** | (−3.0, 17.0) | 100° |

The direction rule ensures that **locations 1 and 2 always share a
left/right hemifield** (same sign of x), and locations 3 and 4 lie in the
other one. The two locations within a hemifield are on opposite sides of the
horizontal meridian. This left/right split is what the code calls the
"hemifield" for the reward manipulation:

- **Hemifield A** = locations 1 and 2 (the side containing the base position)
- **Hemifield B** = locations 3 and 4

**In practice, the base (location 1) is placed on the receptive field of the
recorded neurons**, so location 3 is the mirror location opposite the RF. To
reverse the order in which the RF location experiences the block conditions
(§7.2), the base can instead be placed opposite the RF.

Which physical side is A depends entirely on the base position. With the
default base, A is the **right** hemifield. With the default, locations 2 and 4
are only 3° from the vertical meridian, so their hemifield assignment is
close to the midline.

Edge case: if the base lies exactly on an axis (x = 0 or y = 0), MATLAB's
`sign(0) = 0` makes the rule choose +90°. For example, base (10, 0) produces
location 2 at (0, 10), on the vertical meridian, so the hemifield split
breaks down.

Strobed location values: `targetTheta = round(mod(angle,360)·10)` and
`targetRadius = round(ecc·100)`, plus `targetLocIdx` (1–4).

### 3.3 Windows (important: the parameters are half-widths)
`pds.eyeInWindow` tests `|eye − centre| < WidthDeg` in each dimension, so the
`*WidthDeg`/`*HeightDeg` parameters are **half-widths**:

| Window | Parameters | Effective size |
|---|---|---|
| Fixation | `fixWinWidthDeg = fixWinHeightDeg = 2` | **4° × 4°** square (±2°) |
| Target | `targWinWidthDeg = targWinHeightDeg = 8` | **16° × 16°** square (±8°) around the target centre |

The target window is very generous relative to the ~17° eccentricity, so
endpoint accuracy is only loosely enforced online. Endpoint error should be
analysed offline (`postSacXY` vs. target position).

## 4. Stimuli

### 4.1 Target stimulus types (`stimType`)

| `stimType` | Category | `salience` | `targetColor` | Target appearance | Background (whole screen) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Face image | 0 | 0 | Grayscale monkey face, 6°×6° | Isoluminant grey |
| 2 | Non-face image | 0 | 0 | Grayscale object, 6°×6° | Isoluminant grey |
| 3 | Bullseye | 1 (high) | 1 (hue A) | DKL **0°** rings | DKL **180°** (Δ = 180°) |
| 4 | Bullseye | 2 (low) | 1 (hue A) | DKL **0°** rings | DKL **45°** (Δ = 45°) |
| 5 | Bullseye | 1 (high) | 2 (hue B) | DKL **180°** rings | DKL **0°** (Δ = 180°) |
| 6 | Bullseye | 2 (low) | 2 (hue B) | DKL **180°** rings | DKL **225°** (Δ = 45°) |

**Images (types 1–2).** Images come from `stimuli/gSac_4factors/images.mat`.
Entries 1–15 are monkey faces (15 face textures) and entries 31–150 are
non-face objects (120 textures). Entries 16–30 are not used. On each trial one
exemplar of the required category is drawn **uniformly at random with
replacement**. The image's shape mask makes its surroundings transparent (the
background CLUT index), and its pixel intensities are remapped onto a linear
grayscale ramp (CLUT 18–255, RGB 0→1). Image luminance is **not** matched to
the background.

**Bullseye (types 3–6).** The bullseye is two concentric **square outlines**
(not circles): the outer one is 4° across and the inner one 2° across, both
drawn with a 12 px line, centred on the target location. Colours are in DKL
space at a fixed luminance coordinate (−0.495) and chromatic radius 0.4, so
targets and coloured backgrounds are nominally isoluminant with each other and
with the grey background. Gamma calibration comes from the rig's `LUT_VPIXX_rig#` files.

### 4.2 How salience is created
Salience is the **hue contrast between target and background**. High salience
uses a 180° DKL hue difference and low salience a 45° difference. Target hue
(0° vs 180°) is counterbalanced across salience levels by `targetColor`, so
salience is not confounded with the target's own hue. It **is** confounded
with background hue, since each of the four conditions has its own background.

### 4.3 Background colour as an implicit pre-cue
The background colour is uploaded to the CLUT in `_next.m`, before the trial,
and fills the screen from the **first frame of the trial**, before fixation
onset and target onset. Because the mapping from `stimType` to background is
one-to-one for bullseyes and grey for images, the subject can know before the
target appears:
- whether the target will be an image or a bullseye,
- for bullseyes, the salience level and target hue exactly.

The background does **not** reveal the target location, the reward level, or
face vs. non-face. Between trials, during the joystick-release wait, the
screen shows the previous trial's background.

### 4.4 Experimenter-only display elements
The experimenter sees these through CLUT entries that are background-coloured
on the subject display: the fixation window (grey frame, thickened once
fixation is acquired), the target window (grey frame, thickened on landing),
gaze position (blue square), a 2° grid, and on high-reward trials a **green
frame** at 1.1× the target window. None of these are visible to the subject.

## 5. Trial timeline

### 5.1 Sequence of events (correct trial)

Times are relative to **fixation acquisition (fixAq)** unless stated otherwise.
U(a,b) means a uniform random draw, made once per trial in `nextParams.m → timingInfo`.

| # | Phase (state) | What the subject sees | Requirement | Duration / timing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | `trialBegun` → `waitForJoy` | Trial background colour only | Press and hold the joystick | Up to `joyWaitDur` = 15 s |
| 1 | `showFix` | Fixation point appears | Bring gaze into the fixation window while holding the joystick | Up to `fixWaitDur` = 3 s from fixation onset |
| 2 | `dontMove` (pre-target) | Fixation point | Hold fixation and the joystick | `timeTargOnset` = **U(0.75, 1.0) s** |
| 3 | `dontMove` (target flash) | Fixation point + target | Hold fixation (do not look at the target) | `targetFlashDuration` = **0.4 s**, so the target is on from `timeTargOnset` to `timeTargOnset + 0.4` |
| 4 | `dontMove` (memory delay) | Fixation point only | Hold fixation | Ends at `timeFixOffset = timeTargOnset + U(0.5, 1.0) s`, i.e. 0.1–0.6 s after target offset |
| 5 | `makeSaccade` | Fixation point off (**go signal**), blank screen | Leave the fixation window with a saccade 100–1000 ms after the go signal | `goLatencyMin` = 0.1 s, `goLatencyMax` = 1.0 s |
| 6 | `checkLanding` | Blank | Saccade in flight; landing evaluated when velocity drops below threshold | No explicit limit |
| 7 | `holdTarg` | **Target reappears** at its location (feedback) | Keep gaze in the target window | `targHoldDuration` = **U(0.2, 0.3) s** from saccade offset |
| 8 | `sacComplete` | Target extinguished | Reward is delivered **immediately** | Trial continues for reward duration + 10 ms DAC padding + `postRewardDuration` (0.25 s) |
| 9 | `_finish.m` | Screen cleared to background | Release the joystick | No timeout. The screen flickers with low tones if the joystick is held > 3 s, but only when `passJoy = 0`. |

### 5.2 Derived intervals
- Fixation acquisition to target onset: 750–1000 ms.
- Target onset to go signal: 500–1000 ms (uniform).
- **Target offset to go signal ("memory delay")**: U(0.5,1.0) − 0.4 = **100–600 ms**.
- Fixation acquisition to go signal: 1.25–2.0 s.
- Post-saccade hold: 200–300 ms.

### 5.3 Target reappearance
The target reappears (`targetReillum`) on the first loop in which the state is
`holdTarg`, that is, once the saccade has landed inside the target window. It
stays on through the hold period and is turned off when the state becomes
`sacComplete` (reward). It is therefore visual confirmation of a correct
landing, and it appears only on successful landings. `targTrainingDelay`
(default −1, disabled) would instead turn the target on a fixed time after
the go signal, as a training aid.

## 6. Behavioral requirements and outcomes

### 6.1 Joystick
The code requires the joystick to be held from trial start until reward.
Releasing it in `showFix`, `dontMove`, `makeSaccade`, `checkLanding` or
`holdTarg` ends the trial as **joyBreak**.
`passJoy = 1` on every session, which makes `pds.joyHeld` always return true.
The joystick is therefore not required, and joyBreak / waiting-for-press do
not occur. Setting `passJoy = 0` makes the joystick required.
`p.trVars.passJoy` in the saved data records which applied.

### 6.2 Saccade detection (online)
In `makeSaccade`, on each loop iteration (much faster than frame rate):
- **Anticipatory break.** If gaze leaves the fixation window less than 100 ms after the go signal, the outcome is **fixBreak**.
- **Valid onset.** If gaze is outside the fixation window, between 100 and 1000 ms after the go signal, **and** online gaze velocity exceeds `eyeVelThresh` = 100 °/s, saccade onset is registered and the state becomes `checkLanding`. Online velocity is the RMS of x/y velocity over the last 5 samples (`eyeVelFiltTaps`).
- **Slow drift** out of the window (velocity below threshold) does not count as a saccade. The trial keeps waiting.
- **Too late.** If no valid onset occurs within 1000 ms, the outcome is **fixBreak**.

### 6.3 Landing (`checkLanding`)
The state waits while online velocity is above threshold. Once velocity drops:
1. If any gaze sample since the go signal exceeds 35° in |x| or |y|, the trial is treated as a **blink**: the `blinkDuringSac` code is strobed and the end state is **fixBreak**.
2. Otherwise, if gaze is in the target window (±8°), saccade offset and target acquisition are registered and the state becomes `holdTarg`.
3. Otherwise (landed outside the window), the outcome is **fixBreak**.

### 6.4 Target hold
Gaze must stay in the target window for `targHoldDuration` (200–300 ms, timed
from saccade offset). Leaving the window ends the trial as **fixBreak**.

### 6.5 Outcome table

| End state (`trialEndState`) | Code | Cause | Feedback | Repeated? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sacComplete` | 21 | Correct trial | Juice (high or low duration), no tone | No; the condition is marked done |
| `fixBreak` | 31 | Broke fixation before the go signal; anticipatory saccade (<100 ms); no saccade by 1 s; landed outside the target window; blink; left the target window during hold | Low tone | Yes |
| `joyBreak` | 32 | Joystick released before reward (only possible if `passJoy = 0`) | Low tone | Yes |
| `nonStart` | 33 | No joystick press within 15 s, or no fixation within 3 s of fixation onset | Low tone | Yes |

To tell the `fixBreak` sub-types apart offline, compare `timing.fixBreak` with
`fixOn`/`targetOn`/`fixOff`/`saccadeOnset`/`targetAq`, and look for the
`blinkDuringSac` strobe (2007).

### 6.6 Behavioral measures computed after each correct trial (`calcSacParams`)
From the 1 kHz ADC gaze trace with a 29-tap smoothed differentiator and an
offline velocity threshold of 100 °/s: saccade onset/offset, **SRT** (onset −
`fixOff`), **peak velocity**, and **pre-/post-saccade position** (5 ms means).
These are NaN on error trials.

## 7. Reward and trial-scheduling structure

### 7.1 Reward amounts
| Level | Parameter | Default solenoid open time |
|---|---|---|
| High (`reward = 1`) | `rewardDurationHigh` | 350 ms |
| Low (`reward = 2`) | `rewardDurationLow` | 160 ms |

The trial's value is saved and strobed as `rewardDurationMs`. The reward is
delivered as soon as the hold is complete. Reward is deterministic, so every
correct trial is rewarded at its condition's level.

### 7.2 Session structure: 2 blocks × 2 half-blocks = 4 half-blocks (one cycle = 256 correct trials)

| Half-block | Block | High-probability location | High-reward hemifield | Low-reward hemifield | Is the HP location high-reward? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | **Loc 1** | **A** (locs 1, 2) | B (locs 3, 4) | **Yes** |
| 2 | 1 | **Loc 1** | **B** (locs 3, 4) | A (locs 1, 2) | No |
| 3 | 2 | **Loc 3** | **A** (locs 1, 2) | B (locs 3, 4) | No |
| 4 | 2 | **Loc 3** | **B** (locs 3, 4) | A (locs 1, 2) | **Yes** |

- **Reward** flips hemifield every half-block, always starting with hemifield A high.
- **Probability** is location-specific. Location 1 is high-probability in block 1, and location 3 (opposite location 1, in hemifield B) in block 2. **Locations 2 and 4 are never high-probability.**
- Within each block, the HP location is high-reward in one half-block and low-reward in the other, so probability × reward is crossed at the HP location (last column). Every location, HP or LP, experiences both reward levels within every block.
- The order is **fixed**: half-blocks always run 1→2→3→4 and then the cycle restarts with half-block 1. The order of conditions at the RF is counterbalanced across sessions by where the base location is placed: on the RF (RF location is HP in block 1 and starts high-reward) or opposite it (the RF location becomes location 3).

### 7.3 Trial counts per half-block (64 correct trials)

| | Face | Non-face | Bullseye ×4 types (each) | Total | Share of half-block |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| High-probability location | 10 | 10 | 5 | **40** | 62.5% |
| Each low-probability location (×3) | 2 | 2 | 1 | **8** | 12.5% each |
| **Total** | 16 | 16 | 8 each (32) | **64** | |

- High:low probability ratio per location = **5:1**, within each stimulus category.
- Image vs. bullseye categories are balanced (32/32). Image codes get double reps because there are 2 image codes vs. 4 bullseye codes.
- Within bullseye trials: high vs. low salience 50/50, hue A vs. hue B 50/50.
- **High-reward trial share by half-block.** In half-blocks 1 and 4 the HP location is in the high-reward hemifield, so 75% of trials (40 + 8 of 64) are high-reward. In half-blocks 2 and 3 the share is 25% (8 + 8). This follows from crossing location probability with reward value and is part of the design. It is noted here because it matters when comparing overall reward rate across half-blocks.
- A full cycle is 256 correct trials: 160 at the HP location (80 per block) and 32 at each LP location per block.

### 7.4 Trial selection and error handling (`chooseRow`, `updateTrialsList`)
- `p.init.trialsArray` holds one row per correct trial to be collected (256 rows).
- Before each trial, a row is drawn **uniformly at random** from the rows of the **lowest-numbered half-block that still has uncompleted rows**. The trial order within a half-block is therefore random, and half-blocks never interleave.
- After a **correct** trial, that row is marked done. After **any error**, it stays available, so the same condition (location × stimType × reward) is redrawn **at a random later point** in the same half-block, not immediately.
- A half-block ends only when all 64 of its conditions have been completed correctly. Its length in attempted trials therefore depends on the error rate, and conditions with more errors are over-represented among attempted trials.
- When all 256 rows are done, everything is reset and the cycle restarts at half-block 1. The same array is reused; the randomness comes from the per-trial draw.
- Block boundaries are not signalled to the subject. Only the change in contingencies marks them.

### 7.5 Condition identifiers
- `halfBlock` (1–4) is strobed, and `blockNumber = ceil(halfBlock/2)`.
- `trialCode = 26000 + 1000·block + (targetLocIdx−1)·6 + stimIdx`, which gives **27001–27024** in block 1 and **28001–28024** in block 2. `trialCode` does **not** encode half-block or reward, so use the `halfBlock` or `reward` values.

## 8. What is (and isn't) randomized per trial

| Randomized per trial | Fixed / blocked |
|---|---|
| Which condition row (location, stimType) within the current half-block | Reward level per hemifield (by half-block) |
| Image exemplar (with replacement) | HP location (by block) |
| Target onset time U(0.75,1.0) s | Flash duration 0.4 s |
| Go time after target onset U(0.5,1.0) s | Latency limits 0.1–1.0 s |
| Hold duration U(0.2,0.3) s | Window sizes, eccentricity (= base) |

## 9. Strobed information (summary)

Event codes are strobed at the moment of the event. Visual events (`fixOn`,
`fixOff`, `targetOn`, `targetOff`, `targetReillum`) are queued and sent right
after the screen flip that shows them, and their `p.trData.timing` values are
the flip times. Event codes: trialBegin 30001, joyPress 2001, fixOn 3001,
fixAq 3004, targetOn 4001, targetOff 4003, fixOff 3003, saccadeOnset 2003,
saccadeOffset 2004, targetAq 4004, targetReillum 4006, reward 8000,
fixBreak 3005, joyRelease 2002, nonStart 22004, blinkDuringSac 2007,
lowTone 9001, trialEnd 30009.

At the end of each trial (`p.init.strobeList`), code/value pairs are sent for:
taskCode, date/time, trialCode, blockNumber, rewardDuration (ms), trialCount,
goodTrialCount, vissac (always 0), targetTheta, targetRadius, halfBlock,
targetLocIdx, stimType, salience, reward, targetColor.

> **Ephys caution:** the end-of-trial **reward level** (1/2) is strobed with
> code **8000**, the same code as the reward-delivery event. In the neural
> event stream, an 8000 followed by a 1 or 2 near trial end is the reward
> *level*, not a second reward delivery. `rewardDuration` (18000) also gives
> the level unambiguously. `rewardDuration` is sent twice per trial.

## 10. Timing and display notes
- The frame rate is measured at initialization: 120 Hz on rig 1 and 100 Hz on rig 2.
- The state machine runs once per loop iteration (many times per frame). Drawing happens when a frame is due.
- All `p.trData.timing` values are in seconds relative to `trialStartPTB`.
- There is **no inter-trial timeout** after any outcome. The inter-trial interval is processing time plus the joystick-release wait.
